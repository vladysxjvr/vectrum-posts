E-commerce website development

An online shop combines product information, checkout and the daily work of processing orders. We plan those parts together so customers can understand what they are buying and your team has a clear way to manage the sale. The scope follows your catalogue and business model.

Discuss your project ↗

Catalogue and product discovery

We map categories, product options, availability and the information customers need before buying. Search and filters should reflect the size and structure of the catalogue. Product pages bring together images, descriptions and relevant purchase conditions; content preparation and import are agreed before implementation.

Checkout, payment and fulfilment

We define the checkout steps, customer details and available delivery methods. Payment and shipping integrations depend on the chosen providers, supported countries and merchant accounts. We check successful and failed payment paths, order confirmation and the information passed to the team handling fulfilment.

Tools for running the shop

The administration workflow is planned alongside the storefront: maintaining products, reviewing orders and handling status changes. If you already use an accounting, inventory or CRM system, we examine the available connection and agree which data should move between systems, including how errors are surfaced.

A launch with an agreed scope

Before development, we clarify the product count, currencies, languages, integrations and who supplies commercial and policy content. Acceptance covers mobile browsing, checkout and order handling. The Normobaria project shows a related example of a product and subscription shop within a mobile application.

Questions before starting your project

What affects the cost of an online shop?

Products and variants, catalogue import, design, languages, currencies and connections to payments, shipping, inventory and accounting affect the estimate. Discount rules and order handling matter too. Describe your sales model and existing tools. External subscriptions and payment provider fees need to be included separately in the shop’s operating costs.

What is needed before launching the shop?

Prepare a catalogue with prices, variants, images and stock data, delivery rules and commercial policies. Payment integration requires a merchant account with the chosen provider. Before launch, we check a test order, confirmations and failed payments. Catalogue readiness and external integrations also influence the schedule.
